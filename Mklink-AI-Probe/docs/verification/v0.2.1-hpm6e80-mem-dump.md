# HPM6E80 mem_dump qualification

Date: 2026-09-13. Development branch: `codex/v0.2.1-development`.
Status: candidate C matrix/endurance and final-build matrix, CLI/MCP/Chrome acceptance passed.

## Target and changes

The HPM6E80 hpm6e00evk SDK 1.11.0 flash-XIP Debug fixture was rebuilt with
SEGGER Embedded Studio 8.24, downloaded through the HPM ROM API, and verified
by full readback of 37,328 bytes. The 600 MHz target's 1 ms counter advanced and
its known 4 KiB noncacheable RAM pattern matched.

HPM6E80 shares the JTAG ID used by HPM5301. A successful clock acknowledgement
does not identify an exact part or prove wiring stability. SDK/CLI/MCP/Web now
use an honest qualification description; profile names and defaults are unchanged.
The associated 21 profile/dispatch tests pass.

The probe adds the SDK-defined HPM6E80 AXI SRAM window to the existing bounded
memory fast path. MMIO/OTP remain excluded. The 20 MHz GPIO input sample moves
after TCK CLEAR to fix a previous-bit capture: expected `0x800041b7` was read as
`0x0000836e`. Batch failure diagnostics preserve raw status before cleanup.
4/10/30 MHz shift kernels are unchanged. No RAM-read retry was added.

Probe candidate UF2 SHA-256:

`d14022e5be632ff0eb076bdf9a0f0ad011ffb1f69693f4dda865c34981c4cf68`.

fixed Flash 4 B, fixed RAM 4 B, changing RAM 4 B, contiguous RAM 64 B, RAM 4 KiB,
Flash 4 KiB and four noncontiguous 32-bit variables. RAM 4 KiB ran 15 seconds per
clock; the other cases ran 5 seconds. Every static payload was compared with
known contents; dynamic floats had to remain finite, in range and changing.
CRC, complete-block order, error/drop flags and increasing probe timestamps were
checked. Rates exclude the first 200 ms and include subsequent frame gaps.

RAM word, 120 seconds each RAM/Flash 4 KiB, 30 seconds changing float and
30 seconds four discrete words per profile. Five reconnect cycles then each
exercised all four profiles (20 additional captures). All 30 cases passed.

| Nominal MHz | RAM4B samples / 300 s | RAM4B kSa/s | RAM4KiB blocks/s | Flash4KiB blocks/s | Four-word kSa/s |

| 20 | 25,564,464 | 85.466 | 198.335 | 198.978 | 26.211 |
| 30 | 29,760,480 | 99.501 | 242.410 | 242.706 | 32.782 |

Rates include frame gaps after the 200 ms warm-up. The 30 MHz five-minute
result is 99.501 kSa/s: the short 107.039 kSa/s result is not a sustained

RAM4KiB maximum intervals were 5220/4313 us; these are block timestamps, not

nonmonotonic timestamp or new SBA/DMI fault occurred. USB error/full/blocked
counters remained zero. The historical SBA fault count of one stayed unchanged;
it was not erased from the evidence or called zero lifetime faults.

## Installed runtime and interfaces

The local Skill was updated from development commit `c3c41a9239b6`; all 2,460
packaged files matched the archive and maintainer content was excluded. The GUI
asset identifier remains `7c52b48249a8`, since this change did not rebuild GUI assets.
Actual serial CLI and FastMCP stdio completed 24 operations: all four profiles,
periodic benchmarks and known-word payloads, omitted-profile retention, disconnect
and restoration of the medium default. This is real hardware acceptance, separate
from the 21 software profile/dispatch tests.

Chrome loaded the HPM6E80 ELF and displayed the changing `superwatch_wave[0]`
curve at 20 and 30 MHz. Real Chrome screenshots showed approximately 93.769 and
109.578 kSa/s respectively, with a 200,000-point buffer, 10 us requested interval
and zero displayed transport/backend drops. Those rolling-window GUI values are
not replacements for the independent 300-second endurance rate. The raw harness
requested 1 us; both requests use the existing fast-period semantics.

Applying 30 MHz while 20 MHz acquisition was running stopped the old stream;
restarting displayed the new curve. Applying 4 MHz succeeded, then 10 MHz was
restored and its curve sampled (~64.3 kSa/s window), stopped and disconnected.
The page remains open. Final probe readback: 10 MHz profile, 68,017,799 scans,
zero DMI/SBA faults or recoveries, 391,556 USB starts/completions, zero USB
errors/full/blocked-us. Screenshot export used Chrome automation, not the app's
native PNG/CSV export, which is not newly qualified here.

An automatic approval policy rejected stopping an older WebGUI process. It was
left untouched; acceptance used a separate loopback service on port 8769.

## Preserved failures and limits

The original firmware passed 4/10 MHz baselines but failed at 20 MHz; its results
are not relabeled as passing. During a later wiring check, a 10 MHz dynamic case
failed after 190,488 samples with invalid raw SBA status. It was stopped; the
cause is not established. The subsequent rewired 28-case matrix had no new
SBA/DMI/USB or payload errors. One earlier analyzer capture had no TCK edges,
and an idle capture contained no DMI scans; neither was used for clock results.

HPM5301 has
not been retested with this changed 20 MHz aperture; its previous firmware's
qualification remains separate. No other HPM device, ARM speed, RTT benchmark,
ordinary mem_read/mem_write throughput or permanent OTP write is qualified here.
